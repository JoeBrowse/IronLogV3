What it does

Everything below is in the app already. There is no premium tier that unlocks a second set of features — the subscription simply keeps the app working after the trial.

Muscle readiness

An anatomical body map, front and rear, shaded by what is actually recovered. Every muscle has its own recovery window, and how hard you trained it decides which one applies.

Guided workouts

Pick a split and Iron Log builds the session — ranked by the exercise order you set, weighted toward what has recovered. Or choose every exercise yourself.

Programmes

Multi-week plans you follow day by day, including presets like Push/Pull/Legs and Project Arms. Build your own, reorder any day, swap exercises mid-programme.

Progress charts

Estimated 1RM over time per exercise, grouped by muscle. Machines and variations are tracked separately, so a Smith bench never contaminates your barbell numbers.

Honest logging

Sets, reps, weight, and optionally reps in reserve. Supersets and drop sets. Warm-up ramps calculated from your actual working weight, not a percentage guess.

Two modes

Simple Mode is a set, a weight, and a rep count. Advanced adds RIR, machine tracking, volume analysis and 1RM testing. Switch whenever you like.

The privacy is structural

Plenty of apps promise not to misuse your data. Iron Log is built so that there is no data to misuse — not as a policy, but as an architecture.

  • There is no Iron Log server, and no Iron Log account. Nothing to sign up for and nothing to breach.
  • Your workouts are written to your device's own storage and read back from it. That is the whole data layer.
  • No analytics, no tracking, no ads, no third-party SDKs collecting anything.
  • The one network call is to Google Play, occasionally, to ask whether this account is subscribed. That is it.
  • Backup writes a plain JSON file to your Downloads folder. It is your data in a format you can read.
  • Cancelling the subscription stops the app — it does not take your history. Backup keeps working either way.
